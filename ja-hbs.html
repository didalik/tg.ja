Toll-Gated Jobs, Settled on Stellar

No API keys, no accounts, no subscriptions. A Job Agent -- ja, running this page -- issues its own small asset on the Stellar network and sells one unit of it at a fixed price, the way a toll booth sells passage. Any Job Requester who buys it has paid the toll; the Stellar DEX trade that does the buying is the receipt, a public, timestamped Proof of Purchase (PoP) that costs nothing extra to produce, because the network already produces it as a side effect of the trade itself.

Paying the toll gets you matched with the Agent over a WebSocket, relayed through a single Cloudflare Durable Object using the Hibernation API -- the connection can sit open through an entire job without the Agent paying for idle compute between messages. That's what makes a toll gate like this work at small scale: many short, intermittent jobs, each one billed in fractions of a Stellar fee and a few milliseconds of Worker execution, never in always-on server time.

Delivery gets its own receipt. Before the job even starts, the Agent builds and signs a second Stellar transaction -- one that claws back the very token that opened the gate -- and hands it to the Requester over the same WebSocket. The Requester holds onto it, lets the job run, and only submits it once satisfied: a deliberate, self-funded action that becomes Proof of Delivery (PoD) the moment it lands on-chain. Two Stellar transactions, PoP and PoD, bookend every job -- no escrow, no dispute process, no third party in the middle.

This page is a live Job Requester (jr), running entirely in your browser: it looks up the Agent's current issuer key, places the matching Stellar trade, gets matched over WebSocket, runs a trivial echo job, and submits the Proof of Delivery once it's done. The client-side protocol -- the WebSocket handshake, job matching, and relay -- is daeh, on its way to becoming a standalone npm package.
